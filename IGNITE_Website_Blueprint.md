# IGNITE Leadership Club Website Blueprint

## 1. Website Goal

The IGNITE website should serve as the official digital home for the programme. Its first job is to build trust with government, schools, sponsors, NGOs, parents, and students. Its second job is to make onboarding easier for schools and students. Its third job is to prepare the foundation for future student career guidance and digital learning features.

The website should not look like a simple club page. It should look like a serious youth development initiative that can be trusted by government, schools, CSR sponsors, NGOs, and development partners.

## 2. Main Website Message

The central message should be:

**IGNITE is not a one-time seminar. It is a sustainable youth development system inside schools.**

This message should appear clearly on the homepage, proposal page, and sponsor page.

## 3. Primary Audiences

The website should be designed for five main audiences:

1. Government officials who need to understand why IGNITE supports education, youth development, and social stability.
2. Schools that need to understand how the programme works and how to join.
3. Sponsors and CSR partners who need to see what they are funding and what impact they can expect.
4. NGOs and development partners who need to see the alignment with youth development, SDGs, civic responsibility, and community resilience.
5. Students who need to feel that the programme is practical, inspiring, and relevant to their future.

## 6. About IGNITE Page

This page should explain the story, mission, vision, and why the programme exists.

Recommended sections:

1. Why IGNITE Was Created
2. The Problem We Are Solving
3. Our Vision
4. Our Mission
5. What IGNITE Means
6. Why Schools Matter
7. Why Sustainability Matters

### 1. Why IGNITE Was Created

Suggested copy:

Every year, thousands of students pass their exams but step into adulthood without direction. IGNITE was created to close that gap — bringing structured leadership training, mentorship, and career guidance directly into the school system, so growth does not stop at the classroom door.

### 2. The Problem We Are Solving

Across many Nigerian secondary schools, students receive strong academic instruction but very little structured support for character, leadership, and career decision-making. Left unaddressed, this gap produces graduates who are qualified on paper but unprepared for real responsibility.

### 3. Our Vision

A generation of young Nigerians who lead with discipline, purpose, and integrity — equipped to shape their communities and contribute meaningfully to national development.

### 4. Our Mission

To build a sustainable, school-based system that guides students through leadership training, mentorship, and career orientation, turning potential into practical, lifelong readiness.

### 5. What IGNITE Means

IGNITE reflects exactly what the programme sets out to do: spark the discipline, confidence, and sense of purpose that students carry with them long after they leave the classroom.

### 6. Why Schools Matter

Schools are where habits, character, and direction are formed. Working inside the school system allows IGNITE to reach students consistently, over time, rather than through a single event that fades quickly.

### 7. Why Sustainability Matters

Real change in a young person takes more than one session — it takes a system. IGNITE is built to continue term after term, so schools, sponsors, and government partners can measure lasting impact, not just short-term activity.

## 7. Programme Page

This page should explain how the programme works.

Recommended sections:

1. Programme Overview
2. Who the Programme Is For
3. Delivery Model

5. Weekly Training Areas
6. Role of Teachers and Counsellors

### 1. Programme Overview

IGNITE runs as a structured, in-school programme that combines regular training sessions, mentorship, and practical projects to build student leadership, discipline, and career clarity over a full term.

### 2. Who the Programme Is For

The pilot is designed for secondary school students from JSS3 to SS3 — the stage where career decisions and leadership habits begin to take shape.

### 3. Delivery Model

The programme is delivered through weekly in-school sessions led by trained facilitators, supported by teachers, counsellors, and school liaison officers who help sustain the work between sessions.

### 4. One-Term Pilot Structure

The pilot runs for one school term across two schools, engaging 60 students through weekly training, mentorship, and a closing project that puts what they have learned into practice.

### 5. Weekly Training Areas

Each week focuses on a specific growth area — from self-leadership and communication to SDG awareness, career guidance, and community service planning — building progressively on the last.

### 6. Role of Teachers and Counsellors

Teachers and counsellors support the programme inside the school, reinforcing what students learn between IGNITE sessions. *(Full detail in the programme proposal.)*

### 7. Role of School Liaison Officers

School liaison officers coordinate scheduling, attendance, and communication between IGNITE and each participating school. *(Full detail in the programme proposal.)*

### 8. Role of NYSC SDGs CDS Group

The NYSC SDGs CDS group supports facilitation and community engagement, connecting the programme to national SDG advocacy. *(Full detail in the programme proposal.)*

### 9. Role of Guest Speakers and Mentors

Guest speakers and mentors give students real-world exposure and role models across different career paths. *(Full detail in the programme proposal.)*

### 10. Monitoring and Evaluation

Progress is tracked throughout the term using attendance, participation, and simple assessments, with results compiled into an end-of-pilot impact report.

### Programme Modules

## 18. Visual Direction

The website should feel serious, hopeful, educational, and trustworthy. It should not feel childish, overly corporate, or like a generic NGO template.

Recommended design direction:

- Clean layout
- Strong education and youth-development photography
- Warm amber/gold orange, dark slate/deep charcoal, clean white/off-white, cool gray, and teal/ocean blue
- Clear headings
- Simple icons
- Plenty of real programme evidence as it becomes available
- Sponsor-friendly impact numbers
- Student-friendly language where needed

Official colour palette:

- Warm amber/gold orange as the primary brand accent for title banners, pill badges, highlights, section divider backgrounds, call-to-action emphasis, and key decorative elements
- Dark slate/deep charcoal for major section headers, background cards, text container blocks, footer areas, and primary page titles
- Clean white and off-white for body text areas, light card backgrounds, page canvases, form sections, and crisp contrast
- Cool gray for subtle body text, secondary subtitles, card outlines, dividers, background structural lines, and muted interface details
- Teal/ocean blue as a secondary accent used sparingly for highlight badges, student leadership structures, organizational boxes, and selected trust-building details

Usage guidance:

- Amber/gold orange should carry the IGNITE energy, but it should not dominate every section.
- Dark slate/deep charcoal should create seriousness and credibility for government and sponsors.
- White/off-white should keep the site clean, readable, and professional.
- Cool gray should support structure without making the site feel heavy.
- Teal/ocean blue should be reserved for special system elements, especially student executive council, thematic working groups, dashboards, and structured programme diagrams.

## 25. Target Audience Analysis (/targetlocked)

This section breaks down IGNITE's five core audiences individually — their pain points, desires, objections, the language that lands with them, and what would make each group stop scrolling. Treating all five as one audience produces generic messaging, so website and content copy should be written with the specific group in mind.

### 1. Government Officials (State Ministry of Education, Secondary Education Board, Youth Affairs, and relevant education agencies)

- **Pain points:** Pressure to show results on youth development and security without a large budget; distrust of NGOs that overpromise and disappear after one workshop; accountability to constituents on what is being done for young people.
- **Desires:** A low-cost, low-risk programme they can point to as evidence of action; something that photographs well for commissioning or press; alignment with existing national priorities (SDGs, state development plans).
- **Objections:** Is this sustainable or another one-off NGO project? Who is really behind this — do they have capacity? What does government actually have to commit?
- **Language that lands:** System, sustainable, structured, measurable outcomes, in partnership with the Ministry, civic responsibility, social stability.
- **What stops the scroll:** A hard number tied to their state ("60 students, 2 schools, 1 term — here's what changed") beats any mission statement.

### 2. Schools (Principals, Counsellors)

- **Pain points:** Overstretched staff, discipline problems, students with no career direction, pressure to show value beyond exams without extra budget or workload.
- **Desires:** External support that doesn't cost the school money, doesn't burden already-stretched teachers, and makes the school look progressive to parents and government.
- **Objections:** Will this disrupt our timetable? What if IGNITE stops halfway through the term? Is this safe for our students?
- **Language that lands:** No cost to the school, we bring the structure you provide the students, certificate, reporting, liaison officer.
- **What stops the scroll:** A clear "what you give vs. what you get" comparison — principals decide fast when the ask and the return are both obvious.

### 3. Sponsors / CSR Partners

- **Pain points:** CSR budgets that go into vague goodwill activities with no measurable story to report to their board or shareholders; pressure to show ESG or social-impact numbers.
- **Desires:** A sponsorship package with a fixed price, a clear name-recognition angle, and a story they can put in an annual report or press release.
- **Objections:** Where exactly does the money go? Is there proof of impact, or just promises? Why this programme over the dozens of other requests they receive?
- **Language that lands:** Measurable impact, return on social investment, brand visibility, sponsorship package amounts.
- **What stops the scroll:** A before/after framing — "60 students. 1 term. Here's the report you'll get back."

### 4. NGOs / Development Partners

- **Pain points:** Hard to find credible, ready-to-fund local partners with real documentation rather than just an idea.
- **Desires:** A partner that's already SDG-aligned, already has governance documents in place, and reduces their due-diligence burden.
- **Objections:** Do they have the institutional capacity to absorb funding? Is this duplicating something already funded? What is their M&E framework?
- **Language that lands:** SDG alignment, youth development system, monitoring and evaluation, governance, capacity.
- **What stops the scroll:** Proof of institutional readiness — governance documents, a code of conduct, and an M&E framework already in place or clearly under development — most local youth initiatives an NGO encounters don't show that level of preparation.

### 5. Students (JSS3–SS3)

- **Pain points:** Confusion about what to do after school, pressure from parents to pick a career without real guidance, feeling like school is just exams with no life preparation.
- **Desires:** To feel seen, to get real answers about their future, to belong to something that isn't just another boring school programme.
- **Objections:** Is this just more homework? Will this actually help me or is it just talk?
- **Language that lands:** Direct, personal, future-facing — your future, your strengths, what you're actually good at — not institutional language.
- **What stops the scroll:** A question that hits a nerve, such as asking whether they know what they want to be or are just picking something because everyone else is.

### Messaging Lines by Audience

IGNITE needs two core messaging lines rather than one, because the audiences split into an institutional group (credibility- and sustainability-driven) and a personal group (practical and future-driven).

**Primary line — institutional audiences**

"IGNITE is not a one-time seminar. It is a sustainable youth development system inside schools."

Fits: Government officials, NGOs and development partners, Sponsors and CSR partners. These audiences respond to sustainability, structure, and proof of a system rather than a single event.

**Secondary line — school and student audiences**

"Every student deserves direction, not just instruction."

Fits: Schools (Principals and Counsellors), Students. This line speaks to the practical gap principals already feel in their students, and reads as personal and future-facing to students rather than institutional. It keeps the same short, confident, professional structure as the primary line, so both can sit side by side on the homepage or be used depending on which audience a page or post is speaking to.
